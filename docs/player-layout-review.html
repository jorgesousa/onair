ONAIR · layout review

Where the now bar lives, and when the list steps aside

Two questions, one answer. The now bar is in the right place while you browse, and it costs the picture nothing at normal window sizes. What's missing is a second state for when you just watch: a Watch mode where the list and the bar step aside and come back when you need them. That is the minimal list mode, but as a moment rather than a setting.

Now bar

Keep it docked under the picture while browsing. In Watch mode it becomes the top-left channel card, the same one the TV screen already shows.

Minimal list

Yes, but tied to watching, not a permanent compact rail. Point at the left edge and the list slides back over the picture.

One rule

Chrome never shrinks the picture. If the window is too wide and short for the bar to fit, the bar floats over the picture instead.

01 · WHAT THE DOCKED BAR COSTS

At normal sizes it sits in space that would be black anyway

The rail takes 361 points of width, so the stage is narrower than 16:9. A 16:9 picture leaves black bands above and below it. The now bar is about 118 points tall, and at the default window it fits inside those bands. The picture keeps its full size.

It only starts to cost picture when the window is wide and short. Pick a size: a red outline means the bar is taking picture away.

Today
VStack { video; NowBar } in ContentView.stage. The bar is always docked and opaque.
Rule
If the space above the bar is shorter than a 16:9 picture at full width, float the bar over the picture and let it auto-hide.

02 · THREE PLACES FOR "WHAT'S ON"

The bottom of the picture is already taken

A broadcast keeps its own furniture in the bottom third: subtitles, news tickers, lower thirds. AVKit puts its floating controls there too. A now bar laid over the bottom collides with all of them. The top-left corner is free, and it is where ONAIR's TV screen already puts its channel card.

Keep for browsing

Docked under the picture

Always readable, never covers the picture, and it doesn't move. It holds information you read while choosing a channel, so it should stay still.

Avoid

Floating over the bottom

It's the familiar streaming-app look, but here it sits on top of subtitles and tickers. It also stacks on AVKit's controls, which appear in the same spot with the same mouse move.

Use for watching

Channel card, top left

The information is ours at the top, the controls are Apple's at the bottom, and they show and hide together. It's the same card as on the TV, so the Mac and the TV look alike.

03 · WATCH MODE

The list steps aside while you watch

Today ⏎ makes the window full screen, but the list and the bar stay, so "Full" isn't really full. In Watch mode ⏎ clears the screen: the list leaves to the left and the bar leaves downward, each the way it came.

Nothing is lost. Move the mouse and the card and AVKit's controls appear, then fade after 3 seconds. Press ↑↓ and you change channel without the list; the card shows the new channel at once. Point at the left edge and the list slides back over the picture; it leaves again when you pick a channel or move away. esc brings everything back.

Enter / leave
220 ms ease-out, picture grows. Paired with system full screen, let macOS's own animation carry it, not two at once.
Mouse wakes chrome
Fade in 150 ms, no movement. Hide after 3 s idle with a 260 ms fade.
Zap card
Keyboard, many times a day: appears at once, no slide. Stays 3 s, fades in 200 ms. This replaces the slide-in from proposal 01 in the motion review.
Rail peek
Hover on the left 8 points, 220 ms slide from the left, over the picture with a shadow. It doesn't push the picture.
Browsing. Rail and now bar visible.
// ContentView — one state, two layouts
@State private var watching = false          // ⏎ toggles, esc clears
@State private var chromeVisible = true      // mouse wakes it, 3 s idle hides it
@State private var peekRail = false          // pointer at the left edge

HStack(spacing: 0) {
    if !watching { rail.frame(width: 360).transition(.move(edge: .leading)) }
    stage
}
.overlay(alignment: .leading) {
    if watching, peekRail { rail.frame(width: 360).shadow(radius: 30).transition(.move(edge: .leading)) }
}
.animation(Motion.if(reduce, .easeOut(duration: 0.22)), value: watching)

// stage in Watch mode: the card replaces the bar
if watching {
    PlayerView(player: playback.player)                       // AVKit shows its own controls on mouse move
    if chromeVisible || zapCardVisible {
        TVChannelOSD(channel: c, number: n, now: programme)   // same card as the TV, plus title + progress
            .transition(.opacity)                             // no slide: ↑↓ is a keyboard action
    }
} else {
    VStack(spacing: 0) { video; NowBar(...) }
}
.onContinuousHover { _ in wakeChrome() }                      // restarts a 3 s Task, like TVScreenView.showOSD
04 · THE MINIMAL LIST, CONSIDERED

Why not a permanent compact rail

I looked at three densities. The icon-only rail looks the most minimal but works the worst with IPTV. Providers' logos are often missing or wrong, and ONAIR's numbers are positions in the current list, so 003 is a different channel under ALL than under SAVED. Without the name you can't tell what you're choosing.

Today · 62 pt rows
Keep as the default
Compact · 34 pt rows
Later, as a setting, for long lists
Icons only
Reject: no names, unreliable logos

A compact setting (one line per channel: number and name only, about 1.8 times as many channels on screen) is worth it if someone has thousands of channels. It is a preference, not a mode, so it needs no animation. The search field and filters stay as they are.

05 · WHAT I'D LEAVE ALONE

Rejected along the way

  • RejectAuto-hiding the now bar while browsing. It holds information you read while choosing. Text that disappears under you while you read is worse than a fixed strip.
  • RejectAuto-entering Watch mode after a few idle seconds. The app would decide for you, and the list would vanish while you think. Watch mode starts only with ⏎ or full screen.
  • RejectA rail that collapses to a strip and expands on hover. Every trip to the list becomes a hover wait, tens of times a day. The left-edge peek happens only in Watch mode, where the list is hidden anyway.
  • RejectMoving the now bar to the top of the window while browsing. The title bar and traffic lights are there, and the information would sit far from the list you're reading.
  • WatchTwo bottom bars today. AVKit's floating controls appear just above the now bar. It's fine while docked, but it's why the floating bottom bar fails in Watch mode.

Verdict

Don't move the now bar; add a second state. While browsing, the docked bar is the right call: it's stable, readable, and at normal sizes it uses space that would be black anyway. The gap is watching. Today ⏎ gives you a bigger window with the same chrome.

Watch mode answers both questions with one piece. The list steps aside and comes back from the left edge, the bar becomes the top-left card the TV already uses, and the "never shrink the picture" rule covers wide windows. Build it before proposal 01 of the motion review, because 01's ident is this card.
